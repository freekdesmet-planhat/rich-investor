# Rich Investor: UX audit and punch list

Audit date: 25 Sep 2026 · Prepared by Claude for Freek Desmet · Live doc: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/b6828f0f-15c3-4a9a-ae7a-9a71b2d5d991

## Bottom line

The engine is worth paying for; the product around it is not ready to charge for. The nightly screen, the plain-language "Why" and the Compare matrix are better than what Simply Wall St shows a beginner. But a stranger sees only a login form, the verdict is buried on every screen, search fails on "KO" and "coca cola", and several visible numbers and sentences are wrong. Fix the six blockers in section 9 before taking money.

**What to protect while fixing:** the How it works copy, the Compare matrix, the P/E-against-its-own-history chart, the honesty flags ("forward PEG", "Rests on an approximation"), and the Suggestions screen — which checks the ~293 large caps in the four focus sectors (US + Europe) about weekly, and your watchlist every night. (The earlier "3,422 screened" figure was a looser count than the scan actually walks; corrected in A12.)

**How this was run.** Desktop at 1440 px in your Chrome, using your existing signed-in session (no credentials were in the brief, so the login form was reviewed logged-out but not submitted). Mobile at 390 px via a same-session frame, because the window would not resize. Every screen in the nav, EN and NL. Test footprint: I added Heineken (HEIA.AS), generated one AI summary on it, opened and cancelled the holdings form, then removed Heineken. Your watchlist is back to 32.

## 1. First 60 seconds

A first-time visitor understands nothing about the promise, because there is no page that states it. The root URL redirects to `/login?next=/`.

**What they see (logged out):** "Rich Investor", "A disciplined checklist for long-term investors.", Email, Password, "Leave blank to get a sign-in link by email instead.", "Sign in", "Send sign-in link", "Only approved addresses can sign in." The tagline then repeats in the footer next to "This is a personal analysis tool, not investment advice."

- **Understand:** it is some kind of checklist. Nothing says nine conditions, minutes, patience, or "only buy when every box is ticked".
- **Feel:** a private tool behind a velvet rope. "Only approved addresses" plus "personal analysis tool" reads as a side project, not a product.
- **Do:** leave. There is no sign-up, pricing, demo, screenshot or example stock.

**After login it is not much clearer.** The first desktop viewport is "MARKET CONTEXT" (Buffett indicator 236%, yield curve, index concentration, S&P 500 P/E) and "CHANGED RECENTLY" (11 lines of "1 conditions more"). The watchlist heading starts at y≈650 px. The one stock that passes all nine (ADYEN.AS) sits around row 25, because the default sort is by sector.

**Would they trust it with money decisions?** The tone earns trust ("It is a checklist, not a crystal ball"). The rest spends it: no "who built this", data source only as "Source: yahoo-finance2" in small grey footer text, and visible data errors (section 4).

**What good looks like.** A public landing page at `/`:

- **Headline:** "Know when a great company is on sale. Nine checks, two minutes."
- **Sub:** "Rich Investor checks any large company against nine rules every night. Most days the answer is: wait. When all nine pass, you'll know."
- **Live proof strip:** "We check 293 large companies in the four focus sectors about once a week; your watchlist every night. Today, 2 pass all nine." (Adyen and AppLovin today.) The inputs already exist on your Suggestions page. It sells patience in one line. (Not "3,422 tonight" — that figure was never the true screened set; see A12.)
- **Hero visual:** a real checklist card (Adyen, 9 of 9, each row with its value), not a dashboard screenshot.
- **Three steps:** Add a company you know → See the nine checks → Get an email when all nine pass.
- **CTA:** "Try it on a stock you own" opens a read-only analysis of ASML, Heineken or Apple without an account, then "Start free trial".
- **NL:** "Weet wanneer een topbedrijf in de uitverkoop is. Negen checks, twee minuten."

## 2. Screen-by-screen walkthrough

The pattern across screens: good thinking, wrong order. The answer a novice needs (pass, almost, not yet) is always present but never first.

### 2.1 Login

- **Works:** magic link plus optional password; NL toggle before login.
- **Doesn't:** "Sign in" is the filled primary button even though the helper says to leave the password blank for a link, so the default path uses the secondary button. The tagline appears twice on one short screen. Error states for an unapproved email were not tested (I did not submit the form).
- **Fix:** one email field, primary "Email me a sign-in link", a text link "Use a password instead" that reveals the second field. Move this to `/login`; `/` becomes the landing page.

### 2.2 Watchlist (home)

- **Works:** status chips with counts ("Buy-worthy 1", "Almost there 1", "Watching 30"), sort options, the "forward PEG" honesty badge, "Updated 17h ago" freshness line, visible 2 px focus ring on keyboard nav.
- **Doesn't:**
  - Hierarchy is inverted: macro gauges and a changelog fill the first viewport; the one 9/9 stock is ~25 rows down under "FINANCIAL SERVICES, NON-BANK".
  - "Changed recently" contradicts the rows. AAPL "1 conditions more" while its row history says 6 to 6 since 2026-09-13. NFLX "Almost there → Watching · 1 conditions fewer" while its row shows "▲ 1 since 2026-09-13". Two baselines (last 7 days vs since first added) on one screen tell opposite stories.
  - Pluralisation bug: "1 conditions more" / "1 conditions fewer" on every line.
  - Status rule contradicts your own docs. ADBE "8 of 9 conditions met · missing: growth category" is "Watching"; GOOGL 8 of 9 is "Almost there". Methodology says "one or two short is almost there", yet every 7-of-9 row says "Watching".
  - "○ Not yet reviewed" in amber on all 32 rows, "Needs review 32 · Reviewed 0". Amber for a normal state is noise, and zero reviews by the builder says the review is asked at the wrong moment.
  - The "Watching" status pill has a border like a button; "Remove" is a bare 60×24 px text button with no confirm and no undo. I removed Heineken: it vanished instantly, and the stock page still said "Heineken N.V. is on the watchlist".
  - Sparklines have no label or axis; a flat green line means "no change", which a novice reads as "good".
  - "OUTSIDE THE FOUR FOCUS SECTORS" gets an amber "!" badge, which scolds users for stocks they chose.
  - Market context: Buffett indicator "236%" is amber with no verdict word, while its neighbours say "Normal" and "Broad breadth". All four gauges are US-only for an audience holding Adyen and Hermes. The "?" expands inline and stretches all four cards.
- **Fix, the layout:** (1) a "Ready for your review" strip with a large card per 9/9 stock; (2) "Almost there" as compact cards that name the gap: "GOOGL: needs a 50% fall from its high. Now −14%."; (3) everything else in a compact list sorted by conditions met; (4) market context collapsed to one line: "Market temperature: expensive (Buffett indicator 236%). Expect few stocks to pass."; (5) the changelog collapsed to "This week: 5 moved closer, 6 moved further". Status chips stay; growth and sector filters go behind a Filter button. Remove moves into a "⋯" menu with an undo toast: "Removed Heineken. Undo".

### 2.3 Stock analysis page

- **Works:** the "Why" narrative is the right idea (reasons tied to numbers). Honest caveats: "Rests on an approximation: return on equity and assets.", "PEG: passed on expected growth only", the ROA adjustment note, the tradability note that "it does not affect the verdict". The 5-year chart with the 5y-high line and "−50% entry level" line is a superb teaching device. The P/E help modal is well written and cites source and date. The AI summary has a proper skeleton loader.
- **Doesn't:**
  - Order: a small verdict pill top-right, then a ~350 px text wall, then the chart, then the checklist. On mobile the checklist starts ~1,650 px down.
  - Checklist rows show ✓/✗ and the rule but not the value: "✓ Decline from the 5-year high · at least 50% below..." with no "−68%". Compare shows values; this page should too.
  - Logic bug: Heineken "WHAT PASSES" lists "ROE has stayed above 15% in 0 of the last 4 years (currently 10%) and ROA is 4%." The checklist marks that row ✗.
  - Typo on every decline-short sentence: "short of the 50% requiredr." (Heineken, GOOGL, LOGN.SW).
  - Duplication: "WHAT YOU MUST CHECK" says temporary-vs-structural twice in two sentences; "Read the full reasoning" re-prints the same sentences as one paragraph.
  - Wrong "today": "ADYEN.AS turned buy-worthy today." while its row history says 9 to 9 since 2026-09-13.
  - Freshness is wrong on a new stock: Heineken, analysed seconds earlier, shows "Updated 17h ago".
  - Formatting: GOOGL "market cap $4214.7B" (Compare shows "$4.21T").
  - Two targets per metric: Net debt/EBITDA "Target: ≤ 1 (app default)" and "Checklist pass mark: ≤ 2.5". A novice sees a green dot under a number that misses its "target".
  - "vs. the others you follow: 53% below" on Adyen's 22.3% ROE. The baseline is your watchlist's median, which is dominated by mega caps with very high ROE, so a clear pass of the >15% rule reads as weak.
  - AI summary contradicts the page: "falling 65%", "forward PEG of 0.73", "trailing PEG of 1.20" against the page's 68%, 0.66 and 1.10. The amber "Written against an earlier signal" warning does not stop it being shown. Tone clashes with the app: "a fortress capable of enduring any economic storm", "dirt-cheap forward PEG".
  - The full qualitative review shows on a 4-of-9 stock: "4 of 9 quantitative conditions met. The review below is the final step before a buy decision." It invites people to rationalise a stock the method rejects. "Sell signals" show to non-owners.
  - "Is the problem temporary or structural?" never says what the problem is.
  - Insider activity says "From SEC Form 4" and "No reported insider trades in this window." on Adyen and Heineken, which do not file Form 4. It is a false all-clear.
  - "I own this" opens bright white native inputs on the dark theme, with a "dd/mm/yyyy" date field. It looks unfinished.
  - Header breaks at 1440 px on GOOGL: the long tradability line pushes the verdict pill under "Full research" and "Remove" floats mid-row.
- **Fix, the layout:** a sticky top card: ticker, name, price, then a large 9-dot ring and one headline per state (see section 3). Directly under it the checklist as a grid with values: "✓ Price fall · −68% (needs −50%)". Then the chart. Then "Why" collapsed to three bullets. Then ratios. The review unlocks at 9/9 and previews at 8/9. Rewrites:
  - "the logarithmic-waterfall maths means it needs a 214% gain to reclaim that level" → "After a 68% fall, it has to roughly triple (+214%) to get back to its old high."
  - "declines of 50%+ in quality names are rare, high-conviction entry points" → delete.
  - Review prompt: "Adyen trades 68% below its 2021 high. Is that a temporary setback, or did the business get worse?"

### 2.4 Full research

- **Works:** "Valuation against its own history" (P/E band with 5y low, median, high) and "Why this growth category" (EPS bars with the fitted line) are the most transparent screens in the category.
- **Doesn't:** "cheaper than 5% of the last five years" says the opposite of what it means (P/E 26.0 against a median of 52.1). Adyen's income statement shows 2022 revenue 8.9B and 2023 1.9B with a red "−79.1%", a source mix of gross and net revenue. "A fall this deep is rare at market level: of 17 US market declines..." compares one stock to index crashes and nudges toward buying. News has no dates, and 5 of 8 Adyen headlines are analyst ratings or price targets. Analyst "Target price €1,381.86" is shown. Filings and Transcripts are empty for EU stocks: "No SEC filings" ("EDGAR does not index it") and "No transcripts available for this company."
- **Fix:** "P/E is lower than on 95% of days in the past five years." Hide Filings and Transcripts for non-US listings or link the investor-relations page. Date every news item; hide rating and price-target headlines by default. Drop the market-crash comparison. Flag any year-on-year change beyond ±60% as "Data looks inconsistent" in grey instead of red.

### 2.5 Search and adding a custom stock

- **Works:** autofocus; ticker, name, exchange and country per row; "Not analysed yet"; an "Adding…" state; "✓ On your watchlist · HEIA.AS analysed" about 10 to 15 seconds later.
- **Doesn't:** "coca cola" returns "Nothing matches coca cola." "KO" returns ten LSE and Frankfurt lines (0QKL.L Komax, 0JRL.L Kohl's...) and no Coca-Cola. "apple" returns expired Italian warrants such as "VON CW CALL APPLE 100 A 171221", each with "Add to watchlist". Tiny companies ("Kopy Goldfields") are addable although V1 is large cap only, and no result shows market cap. The empty state is a blank page with one "Watchlist" link. After adding, no verdict appears; the user must find a small "Open analysis" link.
- **Fix:** filter the universe to equities with market cap ≥ $10bn before display; exact ticker match first; normalise spaces and hyphens; primary listing first with other listings grouped ("also on Frankfurt"). Show "$31.5B · Large cap ✓" per row. After adding, show the verdict inline: "Heineken: 4 of 9. Not a candidate now. We'll email you if that changes." Empty state: chips "Try ASML · Heineken · Apple · Adyen" plus "Or see tonight's suggestions". Rename "Find a ticker" to "Find a company".

### 2.6 Suggestions

- **Works:** the most valuable screen for a paying novice: the app does the screening (it checks ~293 large caps in the four focus sectors about weekly; the old "3422 screened names" line overstated the set and was corrected in A12), with Add, Dismiss and "Closest to buyable" sort.
- **Doesn't:** default sort "Newest" puts the only 9/9 (APP) sixth. Each card is a 90-word paragraph. Template bugs: SMCI "operating cash flow covers -305% of net income" labelled "a sign of clean accounting", followed by "Still missing: cash flow quality"; GDDY "The PEG ratio is unknown, above the 1.0 ceiling." Rollins (pest control) is filed under "Luxury goods and consumer discretionary". "(102285 in the universe)" exposes the unfiltered universe. Nothing on the home screen points here.
- **Fix:** default sort by conditions met. Card = ticker, name, nine dots, one line: "Missing: PEG ratio (2.13, needs ≤ 0.7)". Top three on the home screen as "Worth a look tonight". This is the paid hook (section 9).

### 2.7 Compare

- **Works:** the best-designed screen. The matrix of ✓/✗ with values plus "They disagree on 5 of the conditions" is exactly what the stock page checklist should look like.
- **Doesn't:** chips are tickers only ("RMS.PA", "CFR.SW" mean nothing to a novice). In my test, three quick clicks kept only the last selection. The disagreement highlight is a barely visible dark-red tint. Condition names switch to lower case ("focus sector"). "cash flow quality 0.97" has no unit. On mobile the third column is cut off and the "Buy-worthy, over to your qualitative review" pill overflows.
- **Fix:** chips with names ("Hermès · RMS.PA"), a left accent bar on disagreeing rows, units in cells, and stacked swipeable cards on mobile.

### 2.8 How it works (the only onboarding)

- **Works:** the best copy in the product. "It is a checklist, not a crystal ball"; "It is not an instruction to buy"; "A cheap share in a company that is not growing is not cheap, it is just cheap-looking."
- **Doesn't:** it is hidden in the nav, never shown on first login, text-only, and ends in "Start using it" with an empty or unexplained watchlist. Step 2 promises "Almost there means it is one or two conditions short", which the product does not do.
- **Fix:** run it on first login as four screens, each with the real component (checklist card, the three status chips, the chart with the −50% line, the review card). Step five: "Pick three companies you know" with chips, analysed on the spot. Reuse the same four blocks as landing-page sections.

### 2.9 Methodology

- **Works:** "Why the thresholds do not move" and "Fixed is not permanent" are excellent trust copy; the credit to the book is clear.
- **Doesn't:** it never names the four focus sectors or the evidence behind them. "(app default)" on debt is not explained here.
- **Fix:** add the "Why only four sectors?" block from section 5 and link each of the nine rows to its help text.

### 2.10 Account and settings

- **Works:** magic link plus optional password, digest options, "Sign out everywhere", and the JSON export ("your judgement cannot" be fetched again is a lovely line).
- **Doesn't:**
  - Developer copy: "Until an address is saved here, the deployment falls back to the NOTIFY_EMAILS environment variable."
  - "The method states no cutoff for these four" above five groups.
  - "Revenue growth: Good at or below 0.1 / Borderline at or below 0.05". Direction is inverted, and it uses fractions where the app elsewhere says "≥ 10%". "Band, as a fraction" is jargon.
  - The debt pass mark (checklist condition 9) is editable, which contradicts "a rule you relax when a stock you like fails it is not a rule".
  - "Sign out everywhere" uses the same filled blue as Save. There are four separate Save buttons.
  - Missing before you charge: plan and billing, cancel, delete account, privacy policy, terms.
- **Fix:** tabs for Profile, Notifications, Billing, Privacy, and an "Advanced: method settings" tab closed by default. Lock condition 9, or stamp "You changed this rule" on every verdict it touches.

### 2.11 Visual system and accessibility

- **Templated look:** default Tailwind slate (background rgb(2, 6, 23), slate-400 text, blue-400 buttons) plus the Geist font. Clean and consistent, but anonymous, with no logo or mark. A paid money product needs an identity.
- **Colour semantics are overloaded.** Green means "condition met", "rising sparkline" and "Drawdown -68.1%". Amber means "Almost there", "Not yet reviewed", "forward PEG", Buffett 236% and the approximation banner. Define four roles: green = met, red = failed, amber = needs your attention, grey = informational.
- **Type:** section labels are 12 px uppercase; counts and meta text 12 to 13 px. Contrast is fine (slate-400 on slate-950 measures about 7.9:1); size and density are the problem.
- **Tap targets below 44 px:** Remove 60×24, sort links 33×16, mobile menu items 222×32, NL toggle 54×28.
- **Keyboard:** visible focus ring on nav, modals close with Esc. Good.
- **Language control uses a Dutch flag.** Flemish users (your home market) are not Dutch. Use the text "EN | NL".

## 3. Core flow test, timed

The machine side delivers "minutes": about 40 seconds on a clean path from search to verdict. The human side does not: a novice needs another 4 to 5 minutes of reading to know what the verdict means, and the verdict itself is easy to miss.

| Clock (UTC, ~ approximate) | Step | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 17:49:33 | Open Search | Empty page, no examples |
| ~17:49:45 | "coca cola" | "Nothing matches coca cola." |
| ~17:50:05 | "KO" | Ten London and Frankfurt lines, no Coca-Cola |
| ~17:50:25 | "coca-cola" | Works, seven results. Switched to Heineken as a Dutch user would |
| 17:50:51 | Add HEIA.AS | First click showed no change; second showed "Adding…" |
| ~17:51:05 | Analysed | "✓ On your watchlist · HEIA.AS analysed", no verdict shown |
| 17:51:26 | Open analysis | Verdict page loaded: "Watching", 4 of 9 |

Total: 1 min 53 s including three dead-end searches. Then the reading: a ~160-word "Why" (with one wrong bullet), nine checklist rows without values, five ratio cards with two targets each, and a qualitative review that does not apply at 4 of 9.

**Friction points, in order:** search misses on obvious names; no feedback on the first Add click; no verdict after adding; "Open analysis" is a small underlined link; the verdict pill looks like every other pill; the checklist is below the text wall; values are missing from the checklist; jargon ("logarithmic-waterfall maths", "consensus", "PEG").

**Is the verdict unmissable and satisfying at 9/9?** No. Adyen's green pill "Buy-worthy, over to your qualitative review" is the same size and shape as "Watching". Nothing marks that this is rare. The next step ("My qualitative review") is six sections further down.

**What good looks like: three calm states at the top of the page.** Avoid confetti. Robinhood removed its confetti animation in 2021 after regulators criticised gamified trading, and your whole pitch is the opposite of gamification.

| State | Visual | Headline | Line under it | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 of 9 | Nine filled green dots in a ring, card with a soft green edge | "Checklist complete" | "Adyen meets all nine rules. This is rare: 1 of your 32 stocks today. The last step is yours." | "Start my review (5 questions)" |
| 8 of 9 | Eight filled dots, one amber | "Almost there: one thing missing" | "Needs a 50% fall from its high. GOOGL is −14% now; the rule passes below $201.31." | "Email me if it gets there" |
| 7 or fewer | Grey dots, filled count | "Not a candidate right now" | "4 of 9. Missing: sector, growth, price fall, returns, debt." | "Keep watching" (quiet link) |

The price trigger (5-year high × 0.5, so $402.62 × 0.5 = $201.31 for GOOGL) is the most useful number you can add. It turns "wait" into a concrete level and gives people a reason to come back between the rare 9/9 days.

At 9/9, also write a dated line into the stock's history: "Checklist complete on 25 Sep 2026 at €875.50". Over time that becomes the user's own track record, which is what makes patience feel like winning.

## 4. Trust and safety design

The voice is disciplined; the data layer and a few loud phrases undo it. Today the design nudges toward discipline in its copy and away from it in its features.

### "Not financial advice"

- **Good:** stated in the footer on every page, in How it works ("This is an analysis tool, not investment advice.") and above the AI summary ("Not advice, and not a substitute for your own review below."). How it works step 2 is the best disclaimer in the category because it explains, not just denies: "It is not an instruction to buy."
- **Undercut by:** the status word "Buy-worthy"; "high-conviction entry points" in every narrative; the AI's "the valuation finally makes sense"; analyst "Target price €1,381.86"; headlines like "Adyen price target raised to EUR 1,400".
- **Risk to check before launch:** once people pay for it, a per-stock "Buy-worthy" label sent by email may count as an investment recommendation under EU market-abuse rules, which the AFM (NL) and FSMA (BE) supervise. I am not a lawyer; a one-hour opinion from a financial-regulation lawyer is cheap insurance. Renaming the state to "Checklist complete" / "Alle negen gehaald" lowers the risk and matches your own "not an instruction to buy".

### Data freshness

- **Good:** "Updated 17h ago · As of 2026-09-25" on every screen, "Takes effect at the next evaluation" in settings, and "Generated 2026-09-14" plus a stale warning on AI text.
- **Wrong:** a stock analysed seconds ago shows "Updated 17h ago". At 19:48 Amsterdam time, a run 17 hours old predates today's close, yet the label says "As of" today.
- **Fix:** per stock, two facts in plain words: "Price: close of Thu 24 Sep (Euronext)" and "Financials: annual report 2025". Stamp "Just analysed" on fresh ones.

### Source attribution and licensing

- "Source: yahoo-finance2" is the name of an npm package, not a data source. A user needs "Yahoo Finance".
- The bigger problem is legal and operational: that library's own README says "Yahoo does not provide any official API to developers" ([node-yahoo-finance2](https://github.com/gadicc/node-yahoo-finance2)). Charging for a product built on unofficial scraping is a licensing risk and a reliability risk (it breaks without notice). Before launch, pick a provider whose terms allow display to paying users, and price that into the subscription. Most free tiers are for personal use only, so read the display terms, not just the request limits.
- **Where prices actually come from (noted during A12b, 2026-09-26):** live quotes and fundamentals run through the self-hosted **finance-query** service (`FINANCE_QUERY_BASE_URL`), not the `yahoo-finance2` library directly. Two open questions for this inventory item: (1) what does finance-query fetch *upstream*? If it scrapes Yahoo, the licensing point above applies to it too — a proxy does not change the terms on the underlying data. (2) The traffic is carried by finance-query's host IP, so the "unofficial, single-IP" rate-limit exposure sits on that host; our circuit breaker protects it, but a launch depends on that host's stability and terms. Estimates come from FMP (its own free-tier limits). Resolve all three when this item is picked up; no change made now.

### Visible data errors (each one small, together fatal for a numbers product)

| Where | What the user sees | Why it hurts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adyen, Financials | 2022 revenue 8.9B, 2023 1.9B, "−79.1%" in red | A quality company looks like it collapsed |
| SMCI, Suggestions | "-305% of net income" called "a sign of clean accounting" | The sentence contradicts itself |
| GDDY, Suggestions | "The PEG ratio is unknown, above the 1.0 ceiling." | Unknown cannot be above anything |
| GDDY, Suggestions | ROE "currently 443%" passes | Negative or tiny equity makes ROE meaningless |
| Rollins, Suggestions | Filed as "Luxury goods and consumer discretionary" | Pest control; the sector rule is only as good as this mapping |
| Adyen, Heineken | "From SEC Form 4" · "No reported insider trades" | Non-US filers: a false all-clear |
| Adyen, AI summary | 65%, PEG 0.73 and 1.20 vs page 68%, 0.66 and 1.10 | Two numbers for one fact |
| Heineken, Why | ROE of 10% listed under "WHAT PASSES" | Contradicts the ✗ in the checklist |

**Fix:** a sanity layer before anything renders. Outliers (ROE above 100%, a year-on-year change beyond ±60%, negative cash conversion, unknown PEG) become grey "Can't judge reliably", which your Methodology already promises: "A condition with missing data is shown grey rather than failed." Regenerate AI text whenever the signal changes, and hide it until then.

### Patience and not chasing

**Nudges toward discipline (keep):** "A temperature gauge, not a timing tool"; "Watching means... normal rather than a failure"; the Howard Marks question; notes; the nightly (not live) evaluation.

**Nudges against discipline (remove or move):**

- News tab full of analyst upgrades and price targets, with no dates.
- Analyst target price on the research page.
- "A fall this deep is rare at market level: of 17 US market declines...", which frames a drop as a once-in-a-century chance.
- The full buy review open on a 4-of-9 stock, which invites rationalising.
- An editable debt threshold (condition 9).
- "Generate summary" on every stock, which invites shopping for a story.
- A daily changelog of ±1 moves plus 32 amber "Not yet reviewed" flags, which trains daily checking.

**Nudges to add:**

- Price-trigger alerts ("GOOGL passes below $201.31") instead of daily churn.
- Weekly digest as the default; daily as opt-in.
- At 9/9, ask "What would make you sell?" before the review can be saved. Pre-commitment is the cheapest anti-panic tool there is.
- A decision log (bought, passed, price, date) that shows the result a year later. This is the "you can win at this" proof, built from the user's own behaviour.
- A patience line on the home screen: "The method said wait 31 times this week. That's it working."

## 5. The industry condition

Keep it, keep it binary, but take it out of the count and show it as a named lens. Do not weight it: "Nothing is scored or weighted" is your strongest trust line, and a weight would break it.

**Why it needs different treatment.** It is the only condition about what a company is rather than how it is doing. It fails Heineken on identity, not on numbers. The evidence behind it is period-dependent: sector leadership rotates (energy was the best-performing S&P 500 sector in 2021 and 2022, and it is outside your four). And it depends on a classification that is currently leaky (Rollins as "Luxury goods"). Today the app hides all of that: Methodology says only "falls within one of the four focus sectors" and never names them.

**Recommended presentation:**

1. Split the verdict into two parts: "Numbers: 8 of 8" plus a "Focus sector" tag (In focus / Outside focus).
2. States: 8/8 and in focus = "Checklist complete". 8/8 and outside focus = "Passes every number, outside the method's focus sectors" in neutral blue-grey, never a red ✗.
3. One setting, on by default: "Follow the book strictly (sector rule on)". When a user turns it off, every verdict carries "Sector rule off", so the choice stays visible.
   - **Dependency (A12).** The nightly Suggestions scan only walks the four focus sectors — that is what makes its domain ~293 names, not the whole large-cap universe. If this toggle ever ships as a real "sector rule off" mode, the scan's `applyScanScreen` focus filter has to widen with it (and the "we check 293 companies" copy and the ~weekly cadence recompute from the wider domain). Turning the rule off in the checklist while leaving the scan focus-only would mean suggestions never surface the out-of-focus names the setting claims to allow.
4. Replace the amber "!" section header with "Outside the method's four focus sectors".
5. Use GICS-style labels users can look up, and let a user flag a wrong sector.

**The explainer card (Methodology page, and behind every "Focus sector" tag):**

> **Why only four sectors?** The book's research found four sectors that beat the market most consistently: information technology; luxury and consumer brands; media and entertainment; and payments and financial services outside banking. They tend to earn high returns without needing much capital. What the rule can't promise: sector leadership changes. Energy led the S&P 500 in 2021 and 2022. That's why we show it separately. A company outside the four can still pass every number, and the app will say so.

NL: "Waarom maar vier sectoren?" with the same three short paragraphs. Add the study period and source from the book as a line under the card; without it, the claim reads as opinion.

**How competitors handle contested criteria:**

- **Simply Wall St** never uses industry as a gate. Industry is a benchmark inside checks (P/E compared with the industry average), so being in a "bad" industry changes the comparison, not the verdict.
- **Stockopedia** packages contested rules as named "guru screens": each strategy is labelled with its author, its rules are listed, and the user picks which lens to apply.
- **Morningstar** pairs each fair value with an uncertainty rating, so a judgement arrives with its own confidence label.
- **Finviz** leaves sector as a filter the user sets; the tool takes no position.

The common pattern: a contested criterion is an explicit, named lens that the user can see and switch, with the reasoning one click away. That is exactly the "Follow the book strictly" toggle.

### The five changes that most increase conversion to paid and retention

1. **Public landing page plus public demo stock pages.** Conversion today is zero by design: nobody can get in. Public stock pages are also how Simply Wall St gets found.
2. **Verdict-first stock page with a price trigger and alerts.** 9/9 days are rare, so retention depends on a reason to return in between: "GOOGL passes below $201.31. Email me."
3. **Search that works plus "pick three companies" onboarding.** A user with an empty watchlist after minute one does not convert.
4. **Suggestions as the paid hook, with a weekly "near misses" email.** Free: three watchlist stocks and the count ("4 near misses tonight"). Paid: the names, alerts and the digest.
5. **A data trust layer.** Licensed data, sanity checks and correct dates. One visibly wrong number is the fastest way to lose a subscriber in a money product.
